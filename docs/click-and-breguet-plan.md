# The click, and Breguet teeth

Two mechanisms the watch still lacks, and the two things the solver needs
for them. They are one plan because they are two halves of one behaviour:
the crown turned backwards must wind nothing, which takes a click to hold
the ratchet AND teeth that slip so the crown can move at all.

## What is missing

**The click.** Nothing holds the ratchet against the mainspring. Turning
the crown back drives the whole winding train backwards, which since the
reverser the clutches refuse to unwind — the generator simply never does it
([reverser-plan.md](reverser-plan.md)). A real watch has a click: a sprung
pawl that lets the ratchet turn the winding way and holds it the other.

**Breguet teeth.** The rotor still turns the crown, because the clutch and
the winding pinion are geared to each other. Real ones carry sawtooth dogs
that slip — and they slip because the clutch LIFTS along the stem against
its spring as the teeth ride over each other. Nothing expresses that lift.

## The shape of the fix

**A `click` relation: one DOF that may only go one way.** It names ONE mate
and one DOF (as a screw does), a direction it `drives`, and keeps the mark
it is held at in `lead`. After the walk: moved in its direction, the mark
follows; moved against it, the DOF is PUT BACK to the mark — the click
holds — and whatever drove it is left disagreeing, which the residual check
already reports on that relation. That is the honest answer to "the crown
turned backwards": the ratchet does not move, and the relation that tried
says so.

**A freewheel that RIDES OVER its teeth.** Two fields, both optional:

- `teeth`: the dogs' count. The clutch re-engages only where teeth meet, so
  `lead` snaps to a multiple of the pitch — a ratchet's dead band.
- `ride`: `{amount, mate, dof}` — while slipping, that DOF is lifted by
  `amount × frac(lead / pitch)`: zero where the teeth are engaged, full
  just before the next tooth drops in.

The lift is applied in the same pass that reconciles `lead`, AFTER the walk,
so it ADDS to whatever the walk gave that DOF (the yoke's rack gives the
clutch its slide) and nothing propagates from it. That is the whole of
"relations that add" this needs: the freewheel's second output, by
construction additive, rather than a general superposition rule.

**The watch uses both.**

- A click pawl on the barrel bridge, sprung against the ratchet: a `click`
  relation holds the barrel arbor, and a turning cam off it rocks the pawl
  over the teeth as it winds (one sawtooth per ratchet tooth, v102).
- The clutch's dogs become a freewheel with `teeth` = 4 and a `ride` that
  lifts the clutch along the stem far enough to clear them. The crown turned
  back then slips at the dogs instead of unwinding the barrel, and the rotor
  winding no longer turns the crown: the winding pinion runs on ahead of a
  still clutch.
- The dogs are drawn as sawteeth — a ramp per dog, lofted between two radial
  sections — so the geometry says what the relation does. If the loft proves
  fragile they stay square, and the doc says the lift is imposed rather than
  generated by the ramp.

## Verification

- `tests/test_assembly_relations.cpp`: a click lets its DOF run one way and
  holds it the other, keeping the mark across solves and through save and
  undo; a freewheel with `teeth` re-engages only at a tooth and its `ride`
  lifts the named DOF to zero at engagement and full mid-tooth; the ride
  ADDS to what another relation gave that DOF.
- `tests/assembly_relations_smoke.py`: both over RPC, with the refusals.
- The watch: the crown turned back winds nothing and lifts the clutch
  instead of unwinding; the rotor winding leaves the crown still; winding
  forward still winds; the pawl never enters a ratchet tooth it should not;
  the exact sweep and clearances hold with the clutch at its lifted pose.
- Headless and GUI suites; `benchmarks/runners/reference.py`.
