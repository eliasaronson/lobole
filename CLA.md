# Contributor License Agreement

You sign this once, by a comment on your first pull request, which the CLA
check asks for. The notes in [Provenance and decisions](#provenance-and-decisions)
say which published instrument each clause is adapted from.

---

## Why this exists

Loböle is free software under AGPL-3.0-or-later and will stay that way. §2.3
below is a binding promise to that effect: every contribution, including yours,
remains available under the licence in force on the day you contributed. That
promise runs to you personally and you can enforce it.

This agreement additionally lets the maintainer sell commercial licences to
people who cannot accept the AGPL — a company embedding the kernel in a closed
product, or a lab putting Loböle inside training infrastructure. That is the
only reason it exists. It funds the work; it does not close it.

What it does **not** do: it does not take your copyright, it does not stop you
reusing your own code anywhere else, and it does not permit the maintainer to
take Loböle proprietary or move it to a source-available licence. §2.3 forbids
that, and §2.4 puts a deadline on publishing so the promise cannot be honoured
in letter and gutted in practice.

## Provenance and decisions

Each operative clause is adapted from a published instrument.

| Clause | Taken from | Why |
|---|---|---|
| §2.1 | Harmony HA-CLA-I §2.1 (non-exclusive) | What Canonical, Grafana, GitLab and most commercial OSS actually use. The explicit retention in (a) is what makes it comfortable to sign. |
| §2.2 | Harmony §2.2 + FLA-2.0 §3.2 | Harmony's grant with FLA's defensive-purpose revocation, which is the fairer formulation. |
| §2.3 | **Harmony §2.3 Option Five** | The clause that makes dual licensing possible while binding Us to keep the AGPL version available. Harmony grades these One (inbound=outbound) to Five (commercial permitted); this is Five, widened to any OSI-approved licence so We can later move to a permissive licence if that serves the project. |
| §2.4 | The Qt Company / KDE Free Qt Foundation agreement | Qt's twelve-month publication rule, which exists to stop the promise in §2.3 being honoured by freezing an old release while development continues behind closed doors. Without this, §2.3 has a hole. |
| §2.5 | FLA-2.0 §8.2, with a cure period added | Makes §2.3 and §2.4 self-enforcing: nobody has to sue, the rights simply revert. The carve-out for licences already granted protects existing commercial customers from losing their rights through no fault of their own. |
| §2.6 | FLA-2.0 §2.2 and Preamble | Swedish law does not permit a full waiver of ideella rättigheter. FLA states moral rights remain, rather than attempting the waiver Harmony §2.4 uses — the correct treatment here. |
| §3 | Apache ICLA §§4–7, Harmony §3 | (b) is the clause most often quietly false: most contributors are employed under contracts that assign work product. (c) ties into the existing `THIRD_PARTY.md` discipline. |
| §6.3 | Harmony §6.3 | **The successor clause.** Without it, signing contributors to a natural person and later forming a company means re-papering everyone. FLA-2.0 has no equivalent. Cheapest clause here, and only free before incorporation. |
| §6.6 | FLA-2.0 §9.3 | Long-lived project; worth having. |

### Why the licence in §2.1 is non-exclusive

An exclusive licence (FSFE FLA-2.0 §2.1 + §2.3) would let Us act alone against
an infringer of a contributor's portion and leave a cleaner position if the
project were ever sold. It costs goodwill — "exclusive" alarms people — and it
cannot coexist with the retention in (a), which is what makes signing feel
safe. The maintainer owns the bulk of the code, so there is standing to
enforce regardless. Revisit only if selling the project becomes a live
possibility, and before many people have signed.

### What this agreement deliberately does not cover

- **The name and the mark.** "Loböle" and its logo are not licensed under the
  AGPL and are not granted by this agreement. See the trademark note in
  `README.md`.
- **Linkage.** A contribution that statically links an LGPL dependency would
  destroy the licensing option this agreement exists to preserve, and no legal
  clause prevents it. That is enforced in the build — see `THIRD_PARTY.md` and
  the linkage check in `tests/test_conventions.cpp`.
- **The maintainer.** Sole copyright holder when this agreement came into force; signs
  nothing.

### How signing works

`.github/workflows/cla.yml` asks the author of each pull request to sign once,
by a comment, and keeps the signatures on the `cla-signatures` branch. It runs
while the repository variable `CLA_IN_FORCE` is `true`.
